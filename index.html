Gaze lab

This page tracks your gaze with the webcam while you look at images and videos. The camera video and your gaze data stay on this computer and are never uploaded. At the end you download a gaze table, overlay videos, heatmaps and scanpaths.

Use Chrome or Edge on a laptop or desktop with a webcam.

Areas of interest, optional

Define the areas once and reuse them for every participant. The first time, draw the areas on the images and click Export AOI file to save aois.csv. For every later participant, select the same images and click Import AOI file. The session then writes the areas into the gaze table, and aois.csv is also among the downloads at the end.

Combine several recordings

Load the gaze tables of many participants at once. You get pooled heatmaps, scanpaths, area tables and one gaze table with areas for analysis in R.

Check your position

Sit at the distance you entered. Keep your whole face inside the preview at the bottom right. Use even light on your face and avoid a bright window behind you.

What happens next

The session has four parts and takes a few minutes. The screen switches to full screen when you begin.

  1. First you calibrate the tracker.Green circles appear one at a time in different places on the screen. Look at the centre of each green circle and click it five times while you keep looking at it. The tracker learns where your eyes point from the moments when you click.
  2. Second you check the accuracy.The circles are now orange, and five of them appear one at a time. Only look at each orange circle until it disappears, and do not click. You then see how accurate the tracking is. If it is not accurate enough, you can calibrate again.
  3. Third you look at the images and videos.A cross appears in the middle of the screen before each one. Look at the cross until the image or video appears, then look at it freely. Images stay for a fixed time, and videos play to the end. Press S to skip one.
  4. Finally you check the accuracy once more.The orange circles appear again. Only look at them, and do not click. This shows whether the tracking became less accurate while you were looking.

During the whole session

  • Keep your head still and stay at the same distance from the screen. Moving, leaning or slumping makes the tracking less accurate.
  • Keep the light the same. Do not switch lamps on or off or move in front of a window.
  • Stay in full screen and do not switch to other windows.

    Analyse recordings

    This analysis uses the session you just recorded.

    Select the gaze tables, which end in _gaze.csv, and the original images and videos. Files are matched to stimuli by file name. Heatmaps and area summaries pool all participants, and each participant gets a scanpath per stimulus.

    Backgrounds and areas of interest

    Load at least one gaze table to change backgrounds or draw areas of interest.

    To show the plots on your own version of an image, for example one where you marked the areas in Photoshop or Illustrator, choose the stimulus and click Replace background. It should have the same proportions as the original.

    Choose a stimulus and drag on the image to draw a rectangle, then give it a name. Where rectangles overlap, the one drawn later counts. Fixations outside every area are labelled none. Export AOI file saves all areas as aois.csv, and Import AOI file loads it again for the next participant.

    Settings

    Gaze table with areas, for R

    This writes all loaded gaze tables into one table with three extra columns. The column aoi gives the area each gaze sample falls in. The column fixation gives the number of the fixation the sample belongs to, matching the fixation table, and is empty for samples outside fixations. The column fixation_aoi gives the area of that fixation. Images are not needed. Import an AOI table or draw the areas above, and the fixation settings below apply.
